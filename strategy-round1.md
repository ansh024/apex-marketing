## 2. Market research: what's different about selling to plastic surgeons

### The buyer
An owner-surgeon (or practice manager/patient coordinator at larger practices). Cash-pay business. One surgical case = **$6,000–$15,000+ revenue**; a surgeon's lifetime patient value can exceed $20k with revisions, secondary procedures, injectables. This changes the entire ROI conversation vs. dental: **they don't need 34 booked calls a month — they need 4–6 right ones.**

### Their #1 anxiety: lead QUALITY, not lead volume
This came through in every surgeon-side thread and industry source:
- "His agency was sending him tire-kicker leads, and nobody told him."
- "Lead quality > lead volume here every time. Trust is the whole game."
- Agencies drown patient coordinators in price-shoppers and no-shows, then report "leads generated" as a win.
- The dental page's "booked patients not impressions" promise maps perfectly — but for surgeons it upgrades to **"consults with qualified candidates, not form-fills."**

### The patient journey is long — and that's a selling point for the full stack
Cosmetic surgery patients research for **3–12 months**: symptom/desire aware → research procedures → stalk before/afters and reviews → shortlist 2–3 surgeons → consult → often wait weeks more before booking. This is why the service list (ads + SEO + GBP + web dev) is actually coherent: ads capture demand now, SEO/GBP capture the 9-month researchers, the website converts both. The dental page's 4-stage targeting section should be **rebuilt around this journey** — it becomes even stronger here.

### Trust markers are non-negotiable in this vertical
Board certification, before/after galleries, reviews, credible website. Patients are choosing someone to operate on their face/body. Any agency page that looks cheap loses instantly, because…

### The aesthetic bar is brutally higher than dental
The competitor set (Influx, Studio 3, Plastix, MD Consulting) sells to surgeons with **luxury-brand production values** — editorial photography, serif type, muted palettes, "premium positioning." A surgeon's entire product is aesthetics; they judge your taste in the first 2 seconds. **The dental page's utilitarian direct-response look would actively hurt here.** We must look like we understand beauty AND perform like a direct-response page. That tension is the design brief.

### The competitive gap (our opening)
- **Influx / Studio 3:** big, premium, brand-first, 190+ staff, big retainers, slow. They sell *prestige*.
- **Plastix:** results language but leans on vanity numbers ("1,000% traffic increase", "500,000 impressions" — literally the metrics Apex mocks).
- **Nobody in this space** leads with: *money-back guarantee, month-to-month, you own everything, reported in booked consults.* That's a genuinely unoccupied position: **the performance-accountable boutique for aesthetic practices.** David-vs-Goliath framing writes itself.

## 3. The end user & the scroll model

**Who lands here:** an owner-surgeon (45–60, wealthy, time-poor, skeptical, has fired 1–3 agencies) or the practice manager screening for them. Likely on **mobile**, arriving from cold email/DM, a Meta ad, or a referral. They give you one thumb-scroll before deciding whether to keep going.

**Their internal monologue, in order:**
1. "Another agency. What makes you different?" *(0–5 sec)*
2. "Do you actually know MY business — surgical, cash-pay, high-ticket?" *(scroll 1–2)*
3. "Prove it. Show me you understand my patients." *(mid-page)*
4. "What exactly do I get and what does it cost?" *(they WILL look for pricing — transparent pricing is rare in this space and a differentiator)*
5. "What if it doesn't work?" *(risk reversal closes it)*
6. "Fine — but I'm talking to a person, not a sales team." *(founder + booking)*

### Information hierarchy

**MUST be in first 2 scrolls (above ~1400px mobile):**
- Positioning line: surgeons-specific, consults-not-clicks
- The guarantee (60-day money-back) — this is the pattern-interrupt, no competitor says it
- No contracts / own your accounts
- One visual proof element (report mock reframed as "consults booked / cost per consult / revenue per case")
- Single CTA: book strategy call

**Middle (persuasion layer):** agency-burn pain points rewritten in surgeon language → patient-journey targeting philosophy (the "smart" section) → services as one integrated system → founder story → testimonials/case proof (client says testimonials coming; videos available).

**Lower (decision layer):** transparent pricing with value stack → how it works (3 steps) → guarantee restated → FAQ (objections: "we tried ads, leads were garbage", "who owns the ad account", "do you work with my competitors in my city" — exclusivity per market is a huge angle in this niche if client offers it) → final CTA + short form.

**Reveal-later principle:** pricing detail, process detail, FAQ. **Never buried:** guarantee, "consults not clicks," founder accessibility.

## 4. Three creative directions

### Direction A — "The Anti-Agency" (confrontational direct-response, elevated)
**Big idea:** Every agency shows you dashboards. We show you consults.
**Headline territory:** *"Your last agency reported clicks. We report consults booked."* / *"Marketing for surgeons, judged the way you judge results: by outcomes."*
**Copy angle:** Port the dental page's anti-agency voice almost 1:1, upgraded to surgeon vocabulary (consults, cases, patient coordinators, cash-pay). Lead with guarantee. Aggressive, plain, confident.
**Visual angle:** Dark, high-contrast, editorial. Big type as the hero (no stock photo of a surgeon — type IS the visual). Data elements (live-feeling report card) as the only "imagery."
**Motion angle (GSAP):** Kinetic typography hero — words like "impressions / reach / engagement" get struck through as they scroll past, then "CONSULTS BOOKED" locks in. Count-up numbers in the report card. Scroll-pinned objection carousel (their pain → Apex fix flip cards).
**Risk:** Confrontational tone can read as cheap if design isn't immaculate.

### Direction B — "The Practice Growth Operating System" (premium/clinical precision)
**Big idea:** Six services, one machine, one number that matters. Marketing with surgical precision.
**Headline territory:** *"Patient acquisition, built with the same precision you operate with."* / *"One system. Every channel. Measured in booked consults."*
**Copy angle:** Calm authority. The 4-stage patient journey becomes the centerpiece — we map the 9-month cosmetic patient journey and show which service captures which stage (Ads = now, SEO/GBP = the researchers, web = the closer). Guarantee framed as confidence, not dare.
**Visual angle:** Luxury-clinical: warm neutrals/bone/ink or deep green, serif display + clean grotesque, thin rules, generous whitespace. Matches the Influx/Studio 3 bar but cleaner. Client's video assets used as subtle background loops.
**Motion angle:** A scroll-driven "patient journey" line/pipeline that travels down the page connecting stages to services — one continuous SVG path animated with ScrollTrigger. Services light up as the journey passes them. Report numbers assemble at the end of the line.
**Risk:** Can drift too "brand-y" and soften the direct-response edge; must keep guarantee high.

### Direction C — "The Boutique Operator" (founder-led, human, exclusive)
**Big idea:** You wouldn't refer a patient to a 190-person surgery mill. Why hire one for your marketing?
**Headline territory:** *"The marketing partner surgeons actually talk to."* / *"We take on a limited number of practices. One per market. Every one gets Nathan."*
**Copy angle:** Founder story moves UP the page. Anti-big-agency: no account managers, no ticket queues, one person who knows your practice, market exclusivity (verify with client). Guarantee as personal promise, in first person, signed.
**Visual angle:** Warm, editorial, personal — real photography (founder, real reports on a laptop, video assets), handwritten annotations/underlines as a design motif, feels like a letter more than a landing page.
**Motion angle:** Restrained: parallax on photography, annotation marks that draw themselves on scroll, signature that writes itself under the guarantee. Motion supports intimacy, not spectacle.
**Risk:** Depends heavily on founder assets/willingness to be the face; scales the brand around a person.

**My recommendation:** **B as the structural chassis with A's voice injected** — the precision/journey framing earns the premium aesthetic this vertical demands, while the anti-agency copy and guarantee keep it converting like a direct-response page. C's founder section stays as a mid-page trust block (it's already the best-converting pattern from the dental page). I'd pitch A and B to the client, with the hybrid as your recommendation.

## 5. Copywriting angles to develop (whichever direction wins)

1. **The math of one case:** "One rhinoplasty pays for six months of this. That's the entire pitch." — ROI framing unique to high-ticket.
2. **Qualified-consult language everywhere:** never "leads." Consults, candidates, cases.
3. **The coordinator's inbox:** empathy for the patient coordinator drowning in tire-kickers — nobody speaks to this person; she often screens the agency.
4. **"You own everything":** ad accounts, website, GBP, reviews — direct counter to the vendor lock-in horror stories that dominate surgeon forums.
5. **Anti-vanity-metrics glossary:** a small section that "translates" agency reports ("Impressions = people who scrolled past you").
6. **The guarantee, in writing:** render it as an actual signed document/certificate on the page.
